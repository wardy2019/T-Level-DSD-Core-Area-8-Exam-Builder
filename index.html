8.1 Security Risks

Confidential information held by organisations 8.1.1

CategorySpecified examples
Human ResourcesSalaries and benefits; staff personal details.
Commercially sensitive informationClient details; stakeholder details; intellectual property; sales numbers; contracts.
Access informationUsernames; passwords; MFA details; PINs; access codes; passphrases; biometric data.

Why information must remain confidential 8.1.2

InformationSpecified reason
Salaries and benefitsPrevent competitors offering higher wages to attract staff; prevent salary comparison and demands for comparable pay.
Staff detailsProtect privacy; prevent competitors directly contacting staff.
Intellectual propertyPrevent competitors copying designs.
Client detailsPrevent competitors contacting clients; protect client privacy.
Sales numbersMust be protected as commercially sensitive organisational information.
Access informationPrevent unauthorised access.

Impact of failing to maintain privacy and confidentiality 8.1.3

  • Non-compliance with regulations, including possible loss of licence to practise.
  • Loss of trust.
  • Damage to the organisation's image.
  • Financial loss through fines, refunds, lost earnings or terminated contracts.
  • Legal action.
  • Reduced security.
Application chainIdentify the information, explain why it needs protection, then link a failure to a specific impact on the organisation or individual.

8.2 Types of Threats and Vulnerabilities

Technical threats, impacts and mitigation 8.2.1

A threat is something capable of causing harm. A vulnerability is a weakness that can be exploited. A threat may exploit a vulnerability and cause an impact.

ThreatHow it threatens a systemPossible prevention/mitigation direction
BotnetA group of compromised devices can be controlled to carry out malicious activity.Protection software, updates, monitoring and restricting compromise.
DoS/DDoSOverwhelms a service with traffic/requests to reduce availability; DDoS uses distributed sources.Monitoring, traffic controls, resilient capacity and incident response.
Password cracking/brute forceRepeated attempts are used to discover access credentials.Strong access policies, MFA, password management and monitoring.
Cross-site scriptingMalicious script is introduced into content delivered to users.Secure development, input/output controls, updates and testing.
SQL injectionMalicious input attempts to alter database queries/operations.Secure input handling, restricted access, testing and updates.
Buffer overflowData exceeds an allocated buffer and may disrupt or alter execution.Secure development, updates and testing.
VirusMalicious code attaches to files/programs and spreads when activated.Anti-malware, updates, access control and staff awareness.
WormSelf-replicates and spreads across systems/networks.Updates, anti-malware, segregation and monitoring.
KeyloggerRecords keystrokes to capture sensitive information.Anti-malware, controlled software installation, monitoring and MFA.
RansomwarePrevents access to data/systems and demands payment.Backups, updates, protection software, access controls and training.
SpywareSecretly collects information/activity.Protection software, updates, permissions and user awareness.
Remote access trojanProvides unauthorised remote control disguised as legitimate content/software.Anti-malware, application control, training and monitoring.
PhishingDeceptive communication attempts to obtain information or trigger unsafe actions.Training, filtering, verification and reporting procedures.
Spear phishingTargeted phishing tailored to a person/organisation.Targeted awareness, independent verification and controlled disclosure.
SmishingPhishing through text messages.Awareness and verification through trusted channels.
VishingPhishing through voice calls.Identity checking and independent verification.
PharmingRedirects users towards a fraudulent destination.Secure DNS/network controls, certificates and monitoring.
Watering-hole attackCompromises a site/service likely to be used by intended targets.Updates, browser/protection controls, monitoring and threat awareness.
USB baitingMalicious removable media is left to tempt a user to connect it.Training, device controls and restrictions on removable media.
DNS attack/redirectionManipulates name resolution or redirects traffic.Secure DNS configuration, monitoring and protected communications.
Insecure APIWeak API security exposes systems/data or permits unauthorised actions.API certification, access control, testing, updates and monitoring.
Man-in-the-middleAn attacker intercepts or alters communication between parties.Encryption, authenticated services, VPN and secure networks.
Open/unsecured Wi-FiTraffic or devices may be exposed on an untrusted network.VPN, encryption, secure alternatives and user guidance.

Malicious hacking may involve hacktivists, nation states, organised crime or individuals.

Human threats and mitigation 8.2.3

ThreatSpecified prevention/mitigation
Human errorFile properties, confirmation boxes and staff training.
Malicious employeeImmediate removal from premises and immediate suspension of user accounts.
Disguised criminalAccompany visitors and check visitor identification.

Physical vulnerabilities and mitigation 8.2.4

VulnerabilitySpecified prevention/mitigation
Lack of access controlEntry-control systems.
Poor access controlPrevent tailgating; complex access codes; regular code changes; monitor access areas; audit staff access.
Nature of locationProtect against shoulder surfing, environmental threats and vandalism.
Poor system robustnessUse rugged machines where appropriate.
Natural disastersUse physical protection, resilience, backup and recovery measures appropriate to the risk.

Impacts of threats and vulnerabilities 8.2.5

  • Loss or leaking of sensitive data.
  • Unauthorised access to digital systems.
  • Data corruption.
  • Disruption of service.
  • Unauthorised access to restricted physical areas.
Common misconceptionA threat, vulnerability, impact and mitigation are not interchangeable. State each part of the chain accurately.

8.3 Threat Mitigation

Common mitigation techniques 8.3.1

Students must understand the purpose, process, benefits and drawbacks of the following techniques.

TechniquePurpose/processBenefitLimitation/trade-off
Hardware/software security settingsConfigure devices/applications to restrict unsafe behaviour or access.Applies protection at system or application level.Incorrect configuration can reduce protection or usability.
Anti-malwareDetects, blocks, quarantines or removes malicious software.Reduces malware risk.Needs updates and may not recognise every new threat.
Intrusion detectionMonitors activity for signs of attack or policy violation.Supports detection and investigation.May produce false alerts and requires monitoring/action.
HashingCreates a one-way fixed representation used to check integrity or protect stored comparisons.Supports integrity checking without reversible encryption.Not intended to decrypt/recover original data.
Symmetric encryptionUses the same secret key to encrypt and decrypt.Efficient for protecting larger quantities of data.Secure key sharing/storage is required.
Asymmetric encryptionUses a related public/private key pair.Supports secure exchange and identity-based uses without sharing a private key.More processing is generally required than symmetric encryption.
User access policiesDefine permitted access and user responsibilities.Creates consistent expectations and controls.Must be communicated, enforced and updated.
Staff vettingChecks suitability/trust before granting sensitive responsibilities.Reduces some insider risk.Does not guarantee future behaviour and requires resources.
Staff trainingDevelops ability to recognise and respond to risks.Reduces avoidable human error/social-engineering success.Needs reinforcement and does not remove all mistakes.
Software-based access controlRestricts resources/actions according to permissions.Limits unauthorised access.Poorly assigned permissions create risk.
Device hardeningRemoves or disables unnecessary ports, services, software, permissions and access.Reduces attack surface.May remove needed functionality if applied poorly.
BackupsCreate recoverable copies using full, incremental or differential methods and safe storage.Supports recovery after loss, corruption or ransomware.Copies require storage, testing and protection; recovery takes time.
Software updatesApply fixes/improvements to applications and systems.Corrects known vulnerabilities.May create compatibility/disruption risks and needs management.
Firmware/driver updatesApply fixes to device-level software and hardware interfaces.Corrects known device vulnerabilities/compatibility issues.Incorrect or failed updates can affect operation.
Air gapPhysically/logically isolates a system from other networks.Strongly restricts remote access routes.Reduces connectivity/convenience and does not remove local threats.
API certificationChecks/assures APIs against required security expectations.Supports trust in connected services.Certification does not remove the need for ongoing monitoring and secure configuration.
VPNCreates an encrypted private connection over another network.Protects traffic on untrusted connections.Endpoint/account compromise remains possible; performance may be affected.
MFARequires more than one authentication factor.A stolen password alone may be insufficient.Adds user effort and requires recovery/support processes.
Password managerStores/generates credentials in a protected system.Supports strong, unique passwords.The manager/account becomes important to protect and recover.
Port scanningChecks systems for open/listening ports.Identifies exposed services requiring investigation.Results need interpretation; unauthorised scanning may be inappropriate.
Penetration testingTests security by attempting to identify/exploit weaknesses under authorised conditions.Provides evidence of exploitable weaknesses.Requires permission, skill and careful scope; ethical and unethical hacking must be distinguished.

Processes and procedures assuring internet security 8.3.2

ControlSpecified process/reason
Firewall configurationRules for inbound/outbound traffic, traffic types, applications and IP addresses.
Network segregationVirtual, physical or offline network separation limits communication and spread between areas.
Network monitoringObserves network activity to identify issues, unusual behaviour or possible threats.
Port scanningIdentifies exposed ports/services for review and corrective action.
Evaluation focusNo single control removes every risk. Strong answers select layered controls, apply them to the threat/vulnerability and acknowledge realistic limitations.

8.4 Interrelationship of Components Required for Effective Security

CIA triad 8.4.1

ElementSpecified meaningExample controls
ConfidentialityData is kept private by controlling who has access.Access control, encryption, MFA.
IntegrityData has not been tampered with; maintaining confidentiality can support this.Hashing, permissions, audit/monitoring.
AvailabilityData is available and useful; ensuring integrity can support this.Backups, redundancy, monitoring and recovery.

The elements interrelate. For example, poor confidentiality can permit unauthorised changes, harming integrity; corrupted data may no longer be useful, harming availability.

IAAA model 8.4.2

ElementPurposeSpecified techniques/examples
IdentificationRecognises the individual within a digital system.Knowledge-based identification such as username; possession-based methods; biometric-based ID methods.
AuthenticationVerifies the identity claimed during identification.MFA, passwords/passphrases and biometric authentication.
AuthorisationEnsures authenticated users access only permitted resources/actions.Role-based controls and access-control lists.
AccountabilityEnsures actions can be traced to the responsible user.Audit logs and user activity.
Do not confuse IAAA stagesA username can identify a claimed user, a password can authenticate the claim, permissions authorise actions, and logs support accountability.

Revision-Agent Guidance

Strict scopeGenerate questions only from Core Paper 2, Content Area 8, references 8.1.1–8.4.2. Do not introduce unrelated advanced cybersecurity content or operational attack instructions.

Paper 2 question design

  • Map every question to a CA08 reference, command word, mark allocation and AO.
  • Use Section A-style knowledge/understanding questions and Section B-style contextual application, analysis and evaluation.
  • Across repeated sessions, reflect Paper 2 weighting: AO1a 10/90, AO1b 21/90, AO2 38/90, AO3a 12/90, AO3b 9/90.
  • AO2 should be prominent. Students should apply security knowledge to the organisation, system, information and threat in the scenario.
  • Do not force evaluation into every set. Use it only where a meaningful contextual judgement is possible.

Question pathways

  • Information → reason for confidentiality → impact of failure.
  • Threat → vulnerability → impact → prevention/mitigation.
  • Security control → process → benefit → drawback → suitability.
  • CIA element → risk → relevant control → organisational effect.
  • IAAA stage → technique → benefit/drawback → scenario application.

Common misconceptions to detect

  • Threat and vulnerability mean the same thing.
  • Privacy and confidentiality failures only affect money.
  • Phishing, spear phishing, smishing and vishing are identical delivery methods.
  • Anti-malware prevents all attacks.
  • Hashing is reversible encryption.
  • Symmetric and asymmetric encryption use keys in the same way.
  • MFA makes an account impossible to compromise.
  • A backup prevents malware infection rather than supporting recovery.
  • A VPN protects an unsafe endpoint from every threat.
  • Penetration testing is authorised by default.
  • Identification and authentication are the same stage.
  • Authentication automatically grants access to every resource.
  • Confidentiality, integrity and availability operate independently.

Marking emphasis

  • Generic security facts should not receive full AO2 credit without scenario application.
  • Analysis must connect causes, weaknesses, impacts or controls.
  • Evaluation must consider benefits/limitations and reach a supported contextual judgement.
  • Accept valid alternatives only when consistent with the specification and scenario.

TermDefinition

VulnerabilityA weakness that may be exploited by a threat.
MitigationA control/action used to prevent or reduce risk or impact.
ConfidentialityKeeping data private by controlling access.
IntegrityEnsuring data has not been tampered with.
AvailabilityEnsuring data/services are available and useful when required.

HashingProducing a one-way fixed representation of data.

AuthenticationVerifying a claimed identity.
